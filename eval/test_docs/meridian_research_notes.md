# Project Meridian - Research Notes

Kestrel Agricultural Institute - Field Science Division
Study period: Q3 2025

## Study Overview

Project Meridian investigated whether continuous soil moisture sensing could
reduce water use without lowering crop yield on medium-sized farms. The study
ran for 13 weeks, from June 2 to August 29, 2025, across three farms in the
central valley region.

## Equipment

Each managed plot was fitted with an SM-100 soil dielectric moisture sensor
buried 25 cm deep in the root zone. The sensor recorded volumetric water
content every 15 minutes and transmitted readings over LoRaWAN to a local
gateway. Control plots were irrigated on the farm's traditional schedule with
no sensing equipment.

## Experimental Design

The trial used 48 experimental plots in total: 24 sensor-managed plots and 24
control plots, matched in pairs by soil type and crop variety. The plots were
split across three farms: Farm A on sandy loam, Farm B on silty clay loam, and
Farm C on clay. Each plot measured 0.4 hectares and was planted with the same
drought-tolerant maize hybrid.

## Irrigation Protocol

Sensor-managed plots were irrigated only when the average volumetric water
content of the top 40 cm fell below a threshold set at 35 percent of field
capacity. Control plots followed each farm's conventional calendar. Irrigation
events and volumes were logged automatically by the farm gate controllers.

## Results

The average yield improvement in sensor-managed plots was 11.4% compared to
the paired controls, measured as dry grain mass per hectare at harvest. Water
use in sensor-managed plots fell by 23% over the same period. The largest
relative gain appeared on Farm B, where the silty clay loam drained fastest
between irrigation events.

## Early Stress Detection

The most striking observation was an early warning effect: the sensor network
detected the onset of drought stress 9 days before visual symptoms such as
leaf rolling appeared in the control plots. This lead time allowed targeted
irrigation that avoided a projected yield loss during the July heatwave.

## Statistical Analysis

Data were analysed with a mixed-effects model treating farm as a random
effect and irrigation strategy as a fixed effect. The yield difference was
significant at p < 0.01. A sensitivity analysis excluding the heatwave week
reduced the yield improvement estimate to 8.2%, which remains significant at
p < 0.05.

## Funding and Personnel

The work was supported by a $120,000 grant from the GreenAcre Foundation
(grant number GA-2025-114). The lead researcher was Dr. Lena Okafor, with

## Publication

A manuscript describing the trial was submitted to the Journal of Applied
Agronomy in October 2025 under the title "Soil moisture sensing as an early
drought warning system in medium-scale maize farming". A follow-up trial
covering a full growing season is planned for 2026, pending additional
funding.
